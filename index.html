ClayBreeze — A Sustainable Clay-Based Air-Cooling System

Summer heat in Indian homes, classrooms and workshops, with little affordable cooling. ClayBreeze replaces the replaceable cooler pad with porous terracotta pots, kept wet by recirculating water and ventilated by two ESP32-controlled fans. The prototype is fully built and its control logic is running; performance testing is next, so no cooling results are claimed yet.

SEC 02

The problem, and what inspired us

Summer heat in Indian homes, classrooms and workshops is a real problem, and affordable cooling is limited — the Government of India's India Cooling Action Plan notes that India has one of the lowest levels of access to cooling in the world.

During a summer internship at Tinkerers' Lab, Sanjivani University, we looked at what a household, school or workshop can actually use. Air conditioners are expensive to buy and run and use refrigerants with climate impact. Pad coolers do cool, but their pads clog, degrade and must be replaced; grass pads can also grow mould, smell and become a fire risk when dry. Our answer: replace the pad with porous, unglazed terracotta pots — natural, locally made and reusable — kept wet by recirculating water, with ESP32 control of the fans and pump. Low cost, low power and no pad replacement are the design aims; testing will show how well it works.

Cooling approaches compared (summary of project-overview.md)
ApproachPotential advantagesLimitations and considerations
Air conditionersControlled cooling across a wide humidity range; can dehumidify.High purchase and running cost and high electrical demand; refrigerants with climate impact. Out of reach for many households and schools.
Honeycomb / cellulose pad coolersCheaper; large wetted area; established designs that do cool, best in dry air.Pads clog with scale and dust, degrade and need periodic replacement; plastic bodies.
Grass pad coolers (khus / wood-wool)Local, low-cost media; simple construction.Damp pads can harbour mould and bacteria, cause odour and attract insects; combustible when dry; frequent replacement.
ClayBreeze (this prototype)Terracotta pots: natural, locally made, reusable, biodegradable — no pad to replace. The ESP32 runs fans and pump only when needed.Design aims, not results: cooling, water use, power and service life are still to be measured.

Inspiration: Project Vaayu

Reporting on Project Vaayu — a separate student initiative in Delhi that used clay tea cups (kulhads), scrap wood and metal, and water circulation to build cooling units for government-school classrooms — helped inspire our clay-pot approach.

Project Vaayu is not our work, and its outcomes are not evidence of how ClayBreeze performs. Our own measurements will be recorded independently.

The question we are answering

Can a low-cost, low-power cooler that uses porous terracotta pots instead of a replaceable pad — with recirculating water and automatic fan and pump control — produce a measurable reduction in air temperature under defined conditions, with practical water use and safe operation?

SEC 03

How it is meant to work

When water evaporates from a wet surface it absorbs heat. If the clay pots stay wet and air is blown across them, the air leaving the assembly can be cooler — and it is always more humid. The two fans are mounted at the rear on the extension frame: they take in room air from behind and blow it forward through and around the pots, out of the front into the room. Because the pots do not seal against each other or the frame, some air bypasses them; that has not been measured.

Why humidity matters

Direct evaporative cooling has more potential in hot, dry air. As incoming humidity rises, the possible cooling falls, and the air leaving is always more humid than the air entering. We will measure temperature and humidity together.

    SEC 08

    Testing plan

    No results yet. Measured temperature-drop and humidity results will be published here after testing. Below is the test plan for the completed prototype.

    Temperature drop

    Inlet air temperature behind the fans and outlet air temperature in front of the pots, at documented positions. ΔT = inlet − outlet.

    Relative humidity

    Inlet and outlet RH measured at the same time as temperature, because RH depends on temperature.

    Airflow

    Anemometer readings at documented points, if an instrument is available. One point is not total flow.

    Water use

    Reservoir level or mass at the start and end of a timed run; leaks, splashes and refills noted separately.

    Electrical power

    Fan and pump nameplate ratings, plus measured power only where it can be done safely.

    Method. Compare a fan-only run with dry pots against a wetted-pot run, keeping fan setting, sensor positions and duration the same, and recording ambient temperature and humidity for each run. Repeat runs where possible. Raw readings go into data/test-data-template.csv (currently a blank header). Full protocol: testing-and-results.md.

    SEC 09

    Safety and maintenance

    • Secure and terminate all fan and pump cables before powered testing (a loose fan cable is visible in the earlier assembly photographs).
    • Keep the breadboard, ESP32 and relay away from water, splashes and runoff; fit an enclosure.
    • Have any mains wiring and protection checked by a qualified person; isolate power at the source before touching wiring, tubing or pots.
    • Guard the fan blades and check the frame, welds and rods hold the pots without cracking them.
    • Use clean water, drain the reservoir when idle, and clean deposits and slime regularly.
