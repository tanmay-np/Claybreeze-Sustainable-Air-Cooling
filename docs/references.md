# References and Research Context

The sources below inform the background and proposed test plan. They do not establish the performance of the ClayBreeze prototype. Read each source in its own context and avoid transferring reported numbers from another apparatus to ClayBreeze.

1. **The Times of India / ETimes Trending Desk.** “Inside Project Vayu: Delhi students turn kulhads and scrap wood into eco-coolers for underprivileged government school kids.” 8 May 2026. [Article](https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/etimes/trending/inside-project-vayu-delhi-students-turn-kulhads-and-scrap-wood-into-eco-coolers-for-underprivileged-government-school-kids/articleshow/130960612.cms). Media report on the separate initiative that inspired this project; its reported results are not ClayBreeze results.
2. **Action For Nature.** “Amaira Kapoor” — 2026 International Young Eco-Hero Award information describing Project Vaayu. [Award listing](https://actionfornature.org/eco-hero-winners/tag/Terracotta%2BCooling). Project profile, not an independent performance test of ClayBreeze.
3. **Watson, J. A., Gómez, C., Bucklin, R., Leary, J. D., & McConnell, D. B. B.** *Fan and Pad Greenhouse Evaporative Cooling Systems.* University of Florida, IFAS Extension, publication CIR1135/AE069. [Extension guide](https://edis.ifas.ufl.edu/AE069). General explanation of evaporative cooling, wet-bulb temperature, humidity, pad types and maintenance.
4. **El-Said, E. M. S., et al.** “Experimental investigation of earth-air heat exchanger using porous clay vessels for eco-friendly buildings.” *Scientific Reports* 14, article 17548 (2024). [Open-access article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-67212-5). Experimental porous-clay cooling study with a different setup.
5. **Laknizi, A., Ben Abdellah, A., Faqir, M., Essadiqi, E., & Dhimdi, S.** “Performance characterization of a direct evaporative cooling pad based on pottery material.” *International Journal of Sustainable Engineering* 14(1), 46–56 (2021; first published online 2019). [DOI: 10.1080/19397038.2019.1677800](https://doi.org/10.1080/19397038.2019.1677800). Experimental pottery-based evaporative-pad study.
6. **Mittal, A., Kataria, T., Das, G. K., & Chatterjee, S. G.** “Evaporative Cooling of Water in a Small Vessel Under Varying Ambient Humidity.” *International Journal of Green Energy* 3(4), 347–368 (2006). [DOI: 10.1080/01971520600704654](https://doi.org/10.1080/01971520600704654). Study of a porous clay vessel under different ambient humidities.
7. **He, J., & Hoyano, A.** “Experimental study of cooling effects of a passive evaporative cooling wall constructed of porous ceramics with high water soaking-up ability.” *Building and Environment* 45(2), 461–472 (2010). [DOI: 10.1016/j.buildenv.2009.07.002](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.buildenv.2009.07.002). Study of a porous ceramic wall, not the ClayBreeze geometry.
8. **U.S. Department of Energy, Federal Energy Management Program.** “Best Management Practice #13: Other Water-Intensive Processes.” [Guidance](https://www.energy.gov/cmei/femp/best-management-practice-13-other-water-intensive-processes). Guidance on evaporative cooler water use, recirculation, deposits and maintenance.
9. **U.S. Environmental Protection Agency.** “Transitioning to Low-GWP Alternatives in Residential and Light Commercial Air Conditioning.” [EPA guidance](https://www.epa.gov/snap/transitioning-low-gwp-alternatives-residential-and-light-commercial-air-conditioning). Refrigerant and global-warming-potential considerations.
10. **ENERGY STAR.** “Room Air Conditioners.” [Product guidance](https://www.energystar.gov/products/room_air_conditioners). Equipment sizing and energy-efficiency considerations.
11. **Jain, J. K., & Hindoliya, D. A.** “Experimental performance of new evaporative cooling pad materials.” *Sustainable Cities and Society* 1(4), 252–256 (2011). [DOI: 10.1016/j.scs.2011.07.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.scs.2011.07.005). Comparative experimental study of pad materials.
12. **Al-Sulaiman, F.** “Evaluation of the performance of local fibers in evaporative cooling.” *Energy Conversion and Management* 43(16), 2267–2273 (2002). [DOI: 10.1016/S0196-8904(01)00121-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0196-8904(01)00121-2). Experimental comparison of natural-fibre wetted pads and a commercial pad.

## Citation Notes

- Project Vaayu reporting is used only to explain inspiration and motivation.
- Scientific studies on pottery, porous clay or other pad media have different geometries, materials and test conditions. Use them to understand principles and design tests, not to predict ClayBreeze performance.
- Replace or supplement this list if a source link changes or if more directly relevant research is found.
